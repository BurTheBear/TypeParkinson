## Perspective revision — researched camera language

The latest brief asks for a much stronger 3D product launch. Replaced flat device shears with world-space perspective projection, an orbiting camera, extruded rounded chassis/keys and subdivided textures of actual app screens. The rear device treatment is a generic illustration, not hardware created by the team. Geometry is rendered directly into Canvas; no external model, CDN or framework is required.

Camera decisions follow [Sony Cine’s creative breakdown](https://sony-cinematography.com/articles/breaking-down-the-script---part-2---the-creative-breakdown/) and [Adobe’s tracking-shot guidance](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/production/cinematography/camera-shots-and-angles/tracking-shot.html): a move should reveal something, follow the subject and land on a readable composition. These are our applications of those principles, not a prescribed shot recipe from either source.

- 0–7: close camera at an oblique angle, then a crane/pullback reveals the keyboard behind the slipping message. Near and far keys have different projected sizes and motion.
- 14–22: an approximately 140-degree rear/profile/front orbit and pullback reveal the real app; the camera settles for purpose/tools.
- 22–34: orbit the fixed touch target, then settle near head-on while the model becomes clear. Callout anchors are projected from the actual points.
- 34–48: travel across a deep keyboard while glyphs resolve; the corrected final word holds into the cut.
- 48–60: truck through a world-space row of phones at different depths. Hold Today, Practice and Voice between moves.
- 60–70: a smaller orbit/push accompanies the measurement explanation; the screen stays readable.
- 70–80: pull back and fan the record/report apart. Keep the chart and sample disclaimer clear.

Typography stays stable during the strongest camera moves. Every visual pose is a function of absolute time, including the device lighting and video seeks. Reduce Motion opens settled manual poses rather than replaying the camera moves.
